# Lifestyle-brand and commerce research

**SUPPORTING RESEARCH REFERENCES.** These notes preserve strategic research developed during BOBA
Direct planning. They are **not** implementation requirements. BOBA should **not** copy them
literally. They do not amend VISION-1, did not create `D-368` (later allocated to Customer Menu
Read Projection Authority), and do not authorize Wear, Culture, or Drops commerce.

## Why this research exists

The working brand architecture treats Food, Wear, and Culture as expressions of **one brand
world**, not as three unrelated businesses. The research asked how other brands keep food,
apparel, and culture inside a single identity without collapsing them into one inventory/checkout
authority.

## Research references

### KITH / Kith Treats

- Lifestyle brand spanning fashion, food/treats, and collaborations.
- Recurring drop/release mechanics.
- Product categories reinforce the same brand world rather than reading as a restaurant bolted onto
  a clothing store, or the reverse.

**Derived caution:** drop mechanics are a campaign/release language. They are not, by themselves,
pricing, inventory, or checkout authority.

### Aimé Leon Dore

- Retail, café, and community/cultural activity as expressions of one identity.
- Culture expressed through participation/community rather than a generic content page.

**Derived caution:** a Culture tab of articles is not the same as culture people can join.

### Ralph Lauren / Ralph's Coffee

- Food/beverage ritual can extend a master lifestyle world rather than becoming an unrelated
  business.

**Derived caution:** Food can be the ritual core of a lifestyle brand without forcing Wear SKUs
onto the Food Menu, or Food SKUs onto a future Wear store.

### SOCIAL India

- Food/hospitality combined with neighbourhood/community/cultural programming.
- Locally rooted culture can become a meaningful brand property.

**Derived caution:** “local culture” is a participation and programming problem, not a CMS
section title.

## Derived BOBA insight

```text
Culture should be something people participate in,
not merely something the brand claims.
```

A recurring **BOBA-owned cultural ritual / IP** may be more valuable than a generic Culture
content section.

Working examples discussed, **without locking names** and **without making roadmap commitments**:

- Unbothered Sessions
- BOBA Radio
- After Hours
- local creator collaborations
- match/game nights
- Drop Stories

These remain concepts. They are not IMP identities, not CURRENT product, and not
implementation-authorized.

## Mapping onto working BOBA architecture

| Research pattern | Working BOBA reading | Must not become |
|---|---|---|
| One brand world across food / apparel / culture | Food / Wear / Culture pillars | Three disconnected stores |
| Recurring drops/releases | Drops as campaign mechanism spanning pillars | Automatic price/inventory/checkout authority |
| Café / food ritual inside a lifestyle house | Food as current commercial ritual | Unrelated “restaurant site” |
| Culture as participation | owned ritual/IP, local collaboration | Generic Culture page |

See [`brand-architecture.md`](../brand-architecture.md) for the working pillar model and Drop
authority boundary.
